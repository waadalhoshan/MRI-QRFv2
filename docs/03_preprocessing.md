# 3. MRI preprocessing

The same image-processing pipeline is used for OASIS-1 and OASIS-2.

## Step 1 — MRI selection

- OASIS-1: MR1, mpr-1
- OASIS-2: last available visit, mpr-1
- one subject-level MRI observation per participant

## Step 2 — Volume loading and orientation

Analyze-format MRI is loaded with nibabel.

```python
img = nib.load(path)
img = nib.as_closest_canonical(img)
volume = np.squeeze(img.get_fdata())
```

The study does not perform registration, skull stripping, segmentation, or anatomical cropping.

## Step 3 — Robust per-volume intensity normalization

Only finite, non-zero voxels are used to estimate the 1st and 99th percentiles.

For intensity `I`:

```text
I_norm = [clip(I, p1, p99) - p1] / (p99 - p1)
```

Values are clipped to `[0,1]`. Background voxels that were zero in the source are restored to zero after normalization.

## Step 4 — Axial slice selection

Exactly **20 axial slices** are sampled uniformly from the central **40%** of the volume depth, corresponding to approximately 30–70% of the axial dimension.

Each selected z-index is unique.

## Step 5 — 128×128 PNG conversion

Each complete axial slice is:
- retained without anatomical cropping
- resized to `128×128` with bilinear interpolation
- converted to lossless 8-bit grayscale PNG

Expected counts:

| Dataset | Train | Validation | Test | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OASIS-1 | 3300 | 680 | 720 | 4700 |
| OASIS-2 | 2080 | 460 | 460 | 3000 |

## Step 6 — Resolution sensitivity copies

The canonical prepared data are 128×128.

The 64×64 and 32×32 classical-ML inputs are generated by bilinear resizing from the corresponding 128×128 image.

For distorted data, the distortion is generated **once at 128×128** and the distorted result is subsequently resized. The corruption is not re-simulated independently at each resolution.
